# Five "Make Money With AI" Skool Communities: Who Runs Them, What They Teach, How Legit

**How these notes were made (please read first):**
- Skool.com, YouTube, Reddit, Patreon, higgsfield.ai, skoolmakers.com and buildgreatproducts.com were all **blocked** in this research environment (direct fetch failed: DNS / proxy 403). Every finding below comes from **web-search snapshots** of those pages and from third-party sites. Member counts and prices come from search-indexed snapshots whose exact dates are unknown. Treat them as approximate figures from 2026 and re-check the live pages.
- The session's web-search budget ran out before every gap could be closed. Gaps are listed explicitly.
- **URL caveat:** two of the five URLs the user gave (`skool.com/higgsfield` and `skool.com/ai-cash-skool`) **did not appear in any search index**. The closest matches are `skool.com/higgsfield-7889` and `skool.com/ai-cash-skool-6017`. Skool assigns numeric-suffix slugs by default and owners can set a vanity URL, so the user's URLs may be vanity aliases of these same communities. They could also be different communities. Inference only; unverified.
- None of the testimonials or income figures below are independently verified. Each comes from the seller's own sales page unless stated otherwise.

## Q1. Who owns each community, and what is their public track record?

### Takeaway
All five are creator-run. **None is an official company community.** The "Higgsfield" group is *not* run by Higgsfield AI. Jamie Heinrich (AI Filmmaking) has the strongest independently verifiable track record, with decades of film credits. Chris Ashby (product design) and Ania Melnik (web/AI design awards) have moderately verifiable ones. Jason Cooperson's record is mostly self-reported social metrics. Chunquita Hicks has the thinnest public footprint outside her own pages.

## Q2. What exact money model does each community teach?

### Takeaway
There are four distinct models, and all of them are **service or skill businesses**, not passive income:
1. Build and sell apps or SaaS, or build replacement software for businesses (Ashby).
2. Grow and monetize a personal content brand with AI-automated production (Cooperson).
3. Sell AI visual and video production to brands on retainer (Melnik, Heinrich).
4. A grab-bag of AI side hustles plus affiliate and resale income (Hicks).

Several communities also teach, directly or by example, the meta-model of **selling a community or course yourself**.

### Cited Findings
**1. AI App Builders (Ashby): build apps → get paying customers**
- Pitch: "launch an app that gets customers, replaces expensive software or grows your business in 30 days". — [Skool about](https://www.skool.com/aiapps/about)
- Target segments listed: "indie builders looking for customers", "businesses replacing expensive software", and "coaches and creators looking to monetize their audience". — [Skool about](https://www.skool.com/aiapps/about)
- Programs: "Ship in 7" challenge (launch in 7 days) and "Sell in 30" challenge (first customers in 30 days). — [Skool about](https://www.skool.com/aiapps/about)
- Classroom: Claude Code Crash Course, "ProductOS" (idea → customers system), and AI App Design Masterclass. — [Skool about](https://www.skool.com/aiapps/about). AcademyGems calls the system "BuilderOS", so naming may have changed. — [AcademyGems](https://academygems.com/best/claude-code-skool-communities)

### Inferences
- Melnik's and Heinrich's models (AI creative/ad production for brands) are the most concrete and closest to an existing paid market: agencies and brands already buy ad creative and video. Ashby's is concrete but harder, because shipping an app is easy and finding customers is the hard part, which is why he has a separate "Sell in 30" challenge. Cooperson's depends on first building an audience, which is slow and winner-take-most. Hicks's is the least focused. It teaches breadth (twins, music, affiliate, vibe coding) instead of one sellable skill, and two of its income routes (50% affiliate on the community itself; resale "vault" products) earn by recruiting or reselling, not by delivering a service.
- In at least three of five (Heinrich's Premium, Hicks's affiliate program, Ashby's "coaches and creators... monetize their audience" segment), the model drifts toward "sell an online community or course", the same model the owners themselves run.

### Gaps
- Full classroom module lists, lesson counts and update dates were not visible for any community (Skool blocked). Only the "about"-page bullet lists were available.
- Could not see whether Heinrich's base community teaches specific pricing for client AI video work.

## Q7. What do the five have in common, and what should a beginner take away?

### Takeaway
All five sell **access, structure and community around fast-changing AI tools**, at low monthly prices backed by self-reported testimonials. The money models that hold up are **services sold to businesses** (ads and video, apps, content systems). The most consistently profitable activity on display is **running the Skool community itself**.

### Cited Findings
- All five are paid or private Skool groups priced between ~$7 and $75/month, with tiny review samples (4–26 reviews). — [aiapps](https://www.skool.com/aiapps/about); [leveragelab](https://www.skool.com/leveragelab/about); [filmmaking](https://www.skool.com/filmmaking/about); [ai-cash-skool-6017](https://www.skool.com/ai-cash-skool-6017/about)
- AI/Automation is one of the biggest categories on Skool: 94 of the top 1,000 communities by one count, and "dominating the largest communities". This source is promotional and AI-generated, so treat it with caution. — [Joe Apfelbaum Substack](https://joeapfelbaum.substack.com/p/skool-communities-analysis-top-1000)
- At least two explicitly frame the end goal as monetizing an audience, and one (Heinrich Premium) is about scaling a Skool community "in public". — [leveragelab](https://www.skool.com/leveragelab/about); [aiapps](https://www.skool.com/aiapps/about); [filmmaking-premium](https://www.skool.com/filmmaking-premium)
- Third-party reviewers flag that AI video tools change quickly and that "videos that make money" framing is marketing. — [Skoolmakers](https://skoolmakers.com/communities/ai-filmmaking/)
- Value "depends on the specific community". A substantive one can pay off through client work, while a thin one is "a slow drain". — [learningrevolution Skool review](https://www.learningrevolution.net/skool-review/); [AI Funnel Insider](https://aifunnelinsider.com/skool-communities/)

### Inferences
**Beginner takeaways**
1. **Pick one sellable service, not "multiple income streams".** The concrete curricula (Melnik's 8-week path to a retainer, Ashby's ship-then-sell challenges) beat breadth-first "AI twin + music + affiliate + vibe coding" menus.
2. **Fundamentals outlast tools.** Story, sound, camera language, design and sales transfer. Tool tutorials expire in months.
3. **Discount every testimonial.** "$17k in 72 hours" and "$200 → $9k MRR" are survivor anecdotes on sales pages, not typical results.
4. **A low entry price is the hook, not the business.** The ladders run $7 → $97, community → done-for-you studio, BASIC → PREMIUM plus coaching.
5. **Check before paying:** current price (it moves), refund policy in writing ("all sales final" is a flag), whether the owner has verifiable work outside Skool (IMDb, awards, shipped products), and whether "official"-sounding names are actually affiliated.
6. **Much of the tool knowledge is free.** Higgsfield runs its own free Creator Workflow Lab on Discord ([lab.higgsfield.ai](https://lab.higgsfield.ai/)). A cheap community mainly buys accountability, feedback and a peer network.
7. **The meta-lesson:** the owners earn most reliably by selling the community. Teaching a niche AI skill on Skool is itself a model, but it only works after you have results and an audience (see Q8).

### Gaps
- No outcome data (e.g., what percentage of members earn anything) exists for any of the five.

## Q8. What is Skool itself as a business model?

### Takeaway
Skool is a SaaS platform founded by Sam Ovens and heavily promoted by investor and partner Alex Hormozi. Its revenue comes from creator subscriptions, transaction fees and a 40% recurring affiliate program. Gamified "Skool Games" contests reward creators for growing paid revenue. This drives a large supply of "make money with AI / with Skool" communities. Most earn modestly, and a few earn very well.

### Cited Findings
- **Plans:** Hobby $9/month with a 10% + $0.30 transaction fee. Pro $99/month with ~2.9% + $0.30 (3.9% above ~$900 per most sources). Sources disagree on Pro fee details, and one claims no platform fee up to $899. — [Schoolmaker: Skool pricing](https://schoolmaker.com/blog/skool-pricing); [Ruzuku: Skool pricing](https://www.ruzuku.com/compare/skool-pricing); [Learning Revolution fee calculator](https://www.learningrevolution.net/skool-pricing/)
- Break-even between plans is about $1,270/month in membership revenue, by one estimate. — [Learning Revolution](https://www.learningrevolution.net/skool-pricing/)
- **Ownership:** Sam Ovens founded Skool in 2019. Alex Hormozi became an investor and partner in early 2024 and is its most prominent promoter. Ovens retains the product roadmap. — [Tools4Skool: Hormozi Skool](https://tools4skool.com/skool/hormozi-skool); [SkoolPrep](https://skoolprep.com/alex-hormozi-skool); [Hormozi LinkedIn announcement](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/alexhormozi_big-announcement-i-just-made-the-largest-activity-7150924268799717376-hxdr)
- **Affiliate program:** 40% recurring commission on referred creators' platform subscriptions (≈$39/month per Pro referral). Sources disagree on cookie length (14, 30 or 60 days). — [Codingoblin](https://codingoblin.com/blog/skool-affiliate-program); [myskool.co](https://myskool.co/skool-affiliate-program/)
- **Skool Games:** a recurring competition ranking communities by new revenue. Prize descriptions are inconsistent ($30k+, or an LA trip and a mastermind with Hormozi). One winner reported $88,000 in new MRR. A critic calls it "a pyramid scheme on steroids". — [withhimanshu Skool Games review](https://withhimanshu.com/skool-games-review/); [SkoolPrep Skool Games](https://skoolprep.com/skool-games-what-it-is-how-it-works-how-to-win); [CoursePlatformsReview](https://www.courseplatformsreview.com/blog/skool-games/)
- **Community earnings (estimates, upper bound = price × members):** median paid community ≈ $1,217/month. Under 100 members ≈ $500/month; 100–499 ≈ $6,200; 1,000+ members > $40,000/month. — [Profitable.app stats (Oct 2026)](https://profitable.app/skool/stats)
- The top 100 communities total ≈ $11.3M combined MRR, and ≈ $51k MRR is needed to enter the top 100. — [StealWhatWorks](https://stealwhatworks.com/blogs/news/skool-communities-making-real-money-now)
- The median community in a Sept 2026 sample has ~200 members. — [RevenueGeeks Skool stats](https://revenuegeeks.com/software/skool/statistics)
- Premium communities charge up to $10,000/month with 200–1,200 members, per one AI-run analysis. — [Joe Apfelbaum Substack](https://joeapfelbaum.substack.com/p/skool-communities-analysis-top-1000)
- After Hormozi's promotion, hundreds of $99–$999 "make money with Skool" courses appeared. — [Tools4Skool](https://tools4skool.com/reviews/is-skool-a-scam-reddit)

### Inferences
- Skool earns whether or not members succeed: subscriptions, transaction fees, and affiliates recruiting more creators. The Games turn creators' growth into Skool's growth. AI is the hottest niche, so "make money with AI" communities are a natural product for the platform's flywheel.
- For a beginner, running a Skool community is a *second-stage* model. It pays only once you have verifiable results and an audience to sell to. All five owners had prior careers, audiences or YouTube channels before launching.

### Gaps
