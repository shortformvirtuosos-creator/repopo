Research method note (read first): this research was done on 2026-10-09. Direct page fetching (WebFetch/curl) was blocked by the environment's network policy for every host tried (stripe.com, gumroad.com, docs.whop.com, paypal.com, polar.sh, lemonsqueezy docs, etsy, reddit). All findings below therefore come from web-search result excerpts of the cited pages, not full-page reads. Where a fact comes from a third-party or vendor site rather than the platform itself, it is flagged.

## 1. Which low-ticket digital products sell best in 2025–2026 (especially AI-related), with verified revenue examples

### Takeaway
I found only one documented, independently verified revenue figure for a low-ticket template seller: Easlo's Notion and budgeting templates at $239K in a year, from a Business Insider piece dated around 2022. Most other big numbers in this niche are self-reported or repeated secondhand. I could not verify any revenue figures for AI prompt packs, n8n/agent workflow templates, AI video templates or "AI influencer kits".

### Cited Findings
- Jason Ruiyi Chin ("Easlo"), a 20-year-old in Singapore, made $239,000 in a year selling budgeting and Notion template products. This was an as-told-to essay, and Insider says it verified the revenue with documentation. The article appears to date from about 2022, so it is old information. — [Business Insider via Yahoo Finance](https://money.yahoo.com/made-239-000-selling-budgeting-120626735.html)
- Kajabi says Easlo has earned "over $500,000" from template sales. I found no verification for this; it is a secondhand claim. — [Kajabi blog](https://kajabi.com/blog/can-you-make-money-selling-notion-templates)
- Easlo's Gumroad course page says "250,000+ users worldwide" have used his templates. That is a user count (it likely includes free templates), not revenue. — [easlo.gumroad.com](https://easlo.gumroad.com/l/course)
- Small results are common too: one creator's Substack post is titled "How I made 250$ selling Notion templates". — [The Mindful Planner Substack](https://themindfulplanner.substack.com/p/how-i-made-250-selling-notion-templates)
- Platform content restrictions affect which product types are viable. Payhip reportedly bans copyrighted and resold PLR (private-label rights) material, and violations can get an account removed. This is a third-party review. — [Fungies Payhip review 2026](https://fungies.io/payhip-review-2026)
- On a $5 product, fixed per-transaction fees eat a large share of the price. One 2026 Gumroad fee review says a $5 ebook loses about 30% to combined fees (third-party). — [Gumroad fee guides, e.g. checkoutpage.com](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/gumroad-fees) / [ecomm.design](https://ecomm.design/gumroad-review/). The search excerpt did not say exactly which of these pages makes the 30% claim.

### Inferences
- Fixed fees are $0.30–$0.50 per transaction on most platforms (see Section 4), so prices under about $9 lose 20–30% to fees. Fixed fees therefore push sellers toward $9–$49 front-end prices or bundles. My arithmetic: Gumroad's 10% + $0.50 on $5 is $1.00 (20%). If a processor fee of about 2.9% + $0.30 also applies, the total is about 29%, which matches the third-party "about 30%" claim.
- The one verified case (Easlo) is a template business built on a large organic audience and many free templates used as lead magnets. That fits the "attention first, low-ticket second" model, but the figure is old and comes from before the AI-content wave.

### Gaps
- I found no verified 2025–2026 revenue data for AI prompt packs, Midjourney/Sora/Veo video or ad templates, Canva templates, LUTs/presets, AI coloring books/printables, n8n or AI-agent workflow templates, "AI influencer starter kits", or $9–$49/month Skool/Whop communities. The search budget was used up before these could be researched.
- I have no market-wide data (for example Gumroad or Whop category rankings) on which categories sell best in 2025–2026.

## 2. Funnel economics: conversion rates, order bumps, upsells, self-liquidating offers (SLOs), ascension

### Takeaway
The standard SLO math is breakeven CPA = front-end price + (bump price × bump take rate) + (upsell price × upsell take rate). The commonly cited example is $27 + $17×35% + $67×12%, which gives about $40.99 AOV and therefore a $40.99 maximum CPA. Bump take rates are usually quoted at 20–40% and upsell take rates at 5–20%. These benchmarks come almost entirely from vendors selling checkout or funnel software, so treat them as optimistic. The best independent landing-page benchmark (Unbounce 2024) gives a 6.6% median conversion rate, but that figure mixes free opt-ins with purchases.

### Cited Findings
**SLO definition and math**
- An SLO is "a low-ticket product, typically priced between $7 and $100, designed so the revenue from sales covers (or exceeds) the cost of acquiring each customer through paid ads." — SLO guides: [Asset Academy](https://assetacademy.io/offers/offer-types/self-liquidating-offer-explained/), [Growth Models](https://growthmodels.co/create-self-liquidating-offer/), [LTO Ads](https://ltoads.com/blog/how-to-set-up-self-liquidating-offer-funnel). The search excerpt grouped these together and did not say which one gave the exact wording.
- Breakeven formula: "Add up your average order value across all products (main offer + order bump take rate + OTO take rate). That number is the maximum you can pay per customer and still break even." Worked example: $27 product + ($17 bump × 35%) + ($67 OTO × 12%) = $27 + $5.95 + $8.04 = $40.99 AOV, so breakeven CPA is $40.99. I checked the arithmetic and it is correct. — same SLO guide cluster ([LTO Ads](https://ltoads.com/blog/how-to-set-up-self-liquidating-offer-funnel), [Growth Models](https://growthmodels.co/create-self-liquidating-offer/)); exact page not confirmed.
- Counter-example: "if your SLO is priced at $27 and it costs you $35 to acquire a customer through ads, you are losing $8 per sale." — same SLO guide cluster ([Growbo](https://www.growbo.com/self-liquidating-offer-free-lead-gen/), [Stormy AI](https://stormy.ai/blog/self-liquidating-funnel-playbook)); exact page not confirmed.

**Order bumps and upsells**
- One vendor calls a 20–40% bump take rate "healthy". Another says the "lowest conversion rate we've seen for this stage of a funnel is 20%, and the highest would be 60%. On average, the order bump would convert at 40%." — [Data Driven Marketing (tripwire funnel rates)](https://datadrivenmarketing.co/blog/tripwire-sales-funnel-conversion-rates/), [CartFlows](https://cartflows.com/blog/order-bump/). Vendor sources; the excerpt grouped them.
- Other sources put bump acceptance at 15–40% depending on relevance, give "20–35% for bumps and 5–20% for upsells", and target ">10%" for an upsell. — [Thrive Themes](https://thrivethemes.com/order-bump-ideas/), [Tagada glossary](https://www.tagada.io/glossary/order-bump), [systeme.io glossary](https://systeme.io/glossary/order-bump/). Vendor sources.
- Bumps priced at 10–30% of the main product's price are said to convert best. — [Checkout Champ](https://checkoutchamp.com/media/what-is-a-pre-purchase-order-bump-a-101-guide-369) / [Thrive Themes](https://thrivethemes.com/order-bump-ideas/) (vendor).
- SamCart claims that across "$7B+ in processed transactions, sellers who use order bumps consistently see a 30%+ increase in their average order value." SamCart's own worked example contradicts that headline. Its example is 35 bumps at $27 across 100 sales at $97, which equals $945 of bump revenue, or about $9.45 per order. That is roughly a 9.7% AOV lift, not 30%+. — [SamCart order bump guide](https://www.samcart.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-order-bumps)
- AOV-lift claims from other vendors range from "10–30%" to "31% on average" for a single bump. — [ThriveCart multiple order bumps](https://thrivecart.com/features/multiple-order-bumps/), [systeme.io](https://systeme.io/glossary/order-bump/) (vendor; exact attribution not confirmed in the excerpt).

**Landing-page conversion benchmarks**
- Unbounce 2024 Conversion Benchmark Report: the median landing-page conversion rate across all industries is 6.6%; ecommerce is 4.2%, financial services 8.4% and SaaS 3.8%. Visitors arriving from email convert best, at a 19.3% average. Pages written at a 5th–7th grade reading level have an 11.1% median, versus 5.3% at a college reading level. The dataset is 57M conversions, 41,000 landing pages and 464M pageviews. — [Search Engine Land summary of Unbounce 2024](https://searchengineland.com/how-does-your-landing-page-performance-compare-453224); [MarketingProfs](https://marketingprofs.com/charts/2024/52374/landing-page-conversion-benchmarks)
- Unbounce 2020 report: median 3.2% (9.7% as an average); the top 25% of pages had a median of 25%. The datasets differ, so 2020 and 2024 are not directly comparable. — [Unbounce 2020 report](https://unbounce.com/conversion-benchmark-report-2020/)
- I found no newer (2025/2026) Unbounce edition. — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/how-does-your-landing-page-performance-compare-453224)

### Inferences
- Unbounce's "conversion" includes free lead forms, so the 6.6% median overstates paid-checkout conversion for a $7–$47 product on cold social traffic. Plan with lower assumptions and measure your own.
- An illustrative model, built entirely from my own assumptions (not sourced data): 1,000,000 views × 0.5% click to link = 5,000 visitors; × 2% buy at $17 = 100 sales = $1,700. With a 30% take on a $9 bump, add $270. With 8% taking a $47 upsell, add $376. Total about $2,346, or about $23 per buyer. Every rate here is a placeholder to replace with measured data.
- For paid ads: if real AOV after platform fees (Section 4: roughly 5–13% plus fixed fees) and refunds is below CPA, the "self-liquidating" claim fails. The breakeven formula in the guides uses gross revenue, not net margin.
- Bump and upsell take rates often fall as ad spend scales into colder audiences, so blended AOV from a small test may not hold at scale.

### Gaps
- I found no credible benchmark for organic short-form view → profile/link click → purchase rates on TikTok, Reels or Shorts for digital products.
- I found no independent data on ascension rates (low-ticket buyers who later buy mid- or high-ticket).
- I found no verified case study of an SLO funnel's actual ROAS or CPA in 2025–2026.

## 3. Attention engines: AI content at volume, ManyChat comment-to-DM, affiliates, Whop clipping, small or non-English markets

### Takeaway
Whop "Content Rewards" (clipping) lets a seller rent distribution by paying clippers per 1,000 verified views. Rates are reportedly about $0.20–$6 per 1K views, averaging around $1, so about $1,000 buys roughly 1M views at typical rates. Comment-keyword DM automation (ManyChat) is widely used. Its published click-through and conversion figures come almost entirely from vendors and range widely (for example 18–35% DM link CTR, with claims up to 75%). Treat them as unverified.

### Cited Findings
**Whop clipping / Content Rewards**
- How it works: a brand or creator funds a budget, sets a rate per 1,000 views and approves clips that follow its rules. Follower count and prior experience don't matter, because pay follows views. Budgets are finite and first come, first served. — [OpusClip blog](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards); [Shopiator](https://shopiator.com/blog/whop-content-reward-campaigns); [Sprites guide](https://www.sprites.ai/blog/whop-clipping-guide)
- Whop's own example: a $3 rate with a $6 minimum means a video needs 2,000 views before it enters review. — [Shopiator](https://shopiator.com/blog/whop-content-reward-campaigns) / [Clipping Programs](https://clippingprograms.com/clipping/) (third-party, quoting Whop)
- Rate ranges: "$0.20 to $6 per 1,000 verified views, averaging about $1" — [OpenClip guide](https://openclip.app/guides/whop-clipping-guide); "typically $1–$5, averaging around $1.25" — [Ascynd](https://ascynd.io/en/blog/whop-clipping). Low-effort campaigns ("just add this song") pay about $1/1K; original talking-head product videos pay about $3/1K. — [Clipping Programs](https://clippingprograms.com/clipping/)
- Whop's own clipping campaign was reportedly published at $1.25 per 1,000 YouTube views in April 2025. This is older information. — [Ascynd](https://ascynd.io/en/blog/whop-clipping) / [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards)
- A reported 10% platform fee on clipping campaigns could not be confirmed. Views are verified over a tracking window before payout. — [Clipping Programs](https://clippingprograms.com/clipping/)

**ManyChat comment-keyword to DM link**
- ManyChat publishes a setup guide for "comment-to-DM" automation, where a user comments a keyword and receives a DM with a link. — [ManyChat blog](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-quick-automation-comment-to-dm-guide/); [ManyChat keywords 101](https://manychat.com/blog/what-are-keywords-ig/)
- Instagram's messaging rules restrict follow-ups: automated messages should not be sent outside Instagram's 24-hour messaging window. — [ManyChat: Instagram DM automation rules](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-dm-automation-rules/)
- A ManyChat-co-authored study with HypeAuditor compared 3,000 creators using DM automation with 3,000 who don't. Micro creators using automation averaged 46 comments per post versus 18. This measures engagement, not clicks, and is vendor-sponsored. The study is reported in the ManyChat/creator-tool literature, but the search excerpt did not confirm which page hosts it (candidates: [ManyChat keywords 101](https://manychat.com/blog/what-are-keywords-ig/), [Creatorflow case studies](https://creatorflow.so/blog/comment-to-dm-automation-case-studies/)).
- Pentanet holiday campaign (via Unkoa, itself a DM-automation vendor): 75% click-through on automated DMs, 491% ROI, 901% Instagram follower growth in 12 days. Unverified. Unkoa also claims "up to 28%" DM click-through rates. — [Unkoa](https://www.unkoa.com/instagram-dm-automation-vs-email-in-2025-why-manychat-delivers-90-open-rates-and-60-reply-rates/)
- One benchmark source puts DM-link CTR for high-intent recipients at 18–35%. It also gives an illustrative creator example: 847 comments → 698 DM opens (82.4%) → 412 replies → 64 enrollments at $197 = $12,608 from one post. The creator's identity and the data are unverified; treat this as illustrative only. — [Communipass auto-DM statistics 2026](https://communipass.com/blog/auto-dm-statistics-2026-open-rates-conversion-benchmarks/)

### Inferences
- At about $1 per 1,000 views, a clipping campaign costs about $1,000 per million views. At the illustrative funnel rates in Section 2 (about $2,300 revenue per 1M views), clipping could pay for itself only if the offer converts at least at those placeholder rates. It's a testable bet, not a proven one.
- A practical stack would be: AI-produced short-form at volume, a comment keyword such as "PROMPTS", a ManyChat DM with the link, then a low-ticket checkout with a bump. The comment-to-DM step also boosts comment counts, which may help reach (per the vendor study). Neither the reach effect nor the conversion lift is independently verified.
- For a BiH-based creator, Whop clipping is also usable on the earning side, as a clipper, because payouts are per view and follower-agnostic. Whop's BiH payout status is not fully confirmed (see Section 5).

### Gaps
- I found no sourced data on hook formats, posting volume or multi-account strategies that reliably produce millions of views with AI content. I also found nothing on platform policies on AI-generated or duplicate content and multi-accounting (TikTok/YouTube/Meta). The search budget ran out.
- I found no data on what works in non-English or small markets (Bosnian/Serbian/Croatian-language content, regional audience size, or CPMs).
- I could not research affiliate-army models (for example 50% commissions on Gumroad, Whop or Lemon Squeezy affiliates) or collaboration strategies.

## 4. Selling platforms, fees and merchant-of-record (MoR) status

### Takeaway
For a BiH seller who wants someone else to handle EU VAT, three MoR options have published BiH payout support: Gumroad (MoR since 1 Jan 2025, bank payout in BAM), Lemon Squeezy (MoR, BiH on its bank-payout list) and Polar (MoR, payouts via Stripe Connect Express with BiH listed). Whop offers optional tax collection for 2% and has conflicting MoR claims. Payhip collects EU/UK VAT but is not a full MoR, and it pays out only through your own PayPal or Stripe. Etsy and Stan Store look unavailable to a BiH seller.

### Cited Findings
**Gumroad**
- Fees: 10% + $0.50 per transaction for sales through your profile or direct links; 30% when buyers come through the Gumroad Discover marketplace. — [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing)
- MoR: since January 1, 2025, Gumroad handles sellers' sales-tax and VAT collection and remittance worldwide as merchant of record. — [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing). One 2026 comparison article claims Gumroad is not an MoR, which conflicts with Gumroad's own page; Gumroad's page is the better source. — [Gumroad pricing](https://gumroad.com/pricing)
- Third-party fee guides say processor fees (about 2.9% + $0.30) may apply on top, putting the effective take at about 12.9% + $0.80 (13–21%). Fees are not refunded when you refund a customer. — [checkoutpage.com](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/gumroad-fees); [ecomm.design](https://ecomm.design/gumroad-review/). Third-party; this conflicts with descriptions of 10% + $0.50 as all-in, so verify in the dashboard.
- One 2026 fee guide reports that Gumroad raised its standard minimum payout from $10 to $100 in March 2026. — [roo.beehiiv.com Gumroad fees 2026](https://roo.beehiiv.com/p/gumroad-fees-2026) / [checkoutpage.com](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/gumroad-fees) (third-party; exact page not confirmed)

**Whop**
- Fees: domestic cards 2.7% + $0.30; international cards +1.5%; currency conversion +1%; ACH 1.5% (max $5); no setup or monthly fees; subscription billing 0.5% per transaction when enabled. — [Whop fees docs](https://docs.whop.com/fees); [Whop blog](https://whop.com/blog/payment-processors/)
- Tax: there are three tax options for direct sales. Two "managed" options cost 2% per transaction; "I'll handle tax myself" has no Whop tax fee. Whop collects and remits VAT for Discover sales and when you choose "Whop collects and remits tax". Whop maintains EU and UK VAT registrations. — [Whop Docs: Taxes](https://docs.whop.com/payments-and-billing/fees/taxes)
- Whop's own pages conflict on MoR status. Marketing pages say Whop "acts as your Merchant of Record for card network rules and payment settlement". A Whop comparison article says "Whop isn't a merchant of record – you stay the seller, with tax collection and remittance available as an add-on." — [Whop blog: ecommerce payment processing](https://whop.com/blog/ecommerce-payment-processing/); [Whop blog: Dodo Payments review](https://whop.com/blog/dodo-payments-review/)
- Whop's own example: on a $100 US one-time sale, Whop's fee is $3.00, or $5.00 with the tax add-on (Whop's own comparison, so possibly biased). — [Whop blog: Dodo Payments review](https://whop.com/blog/dodo-payments-review/)

**Lemon Squeezy**
- Fee reported at 5% + 50¢ (third-party review title). — [Dodo Payments' Lemon Squeezy review 2026](https://dodopayments.com/blogs/lemonsqueezy-review)
- Lemon Squeezy's changelog notes a 2026 update: "Lemon Squeezy + Stripe Managed Payments". Lemon Squeezy is now Stripe-owned, so its terms and country lists may change. — [Lemon Squeezy changelog](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/changelog?ef124dd3_page=15)
- Payout schedule: payouts are created on the 1st and 15th, become available on the 14th and 28th, and take 1–5 days to arrive. — [Lemon Squeezy docs: Getting paid](https://docs.lemonsqueezy.com/help/getting-started/getting-paid)
- Payout fee: 0% for US bank accounts and 1% for non-US bank accounts. This comes from a blog post roughly two years old (about late 2024), so it may be outdated. — [Lemon Squeezy blog: payout fees sliced](https://www.lemonsqueezy.com/blog/payout-fees-sliced). A third party (Payhip) claims 0.5% + $2.50, rising to 2.5% + $2.50 outside the US, which is likely older. — [Payhip hub](https://payhip.com/hub/what-is-lemon-squeezy/)

**Polar**
- MoR: Polar collects payments from customers and transfers the balance, net of fees, to your payout account. It handles VAT and similar indirect taxes; you remain responsible for personal income tax. — [Polar docs: Payment & Taxes](https://docs.polar.sh/payment); [Polar MoR intro](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/introduction)
- Fees: 4% + 40¢ per transaction, which includes Stripe's 2.9% + 30¢. Add +1.5% for international (non-US) cards and +0.5% for subscriptions. There is no markup on payouts, but Stripe payout costs apply: $2 per month with an active payout, plus 0.25% + $0.25 per payout, and 0.25–1% currency conversion. Withdrawals are manual. — [Polar fees](https://polar.sh/docs/merchant-of-record/fees); [Polar payouts](https://polar.sh/docs/features/finance/payouts)
- Minimum payout is $10 by default. — [Polar payouts](https://polar.sh/docs/features/finance/payouts)

**Payhip**
- Plans: Free ($0 + 5%), Plus ($29/mo + 2%), Pro ($99/mo + 0%), with processor fees (about 2.9% + $0.30) on top. — [Fungies Payhip review 2026](https://fungies.io/payhip-review-2026); [Fungies Sellfy vs Payhip vs Gumroad](https://fungies.io/sellfy-vs-payhip-vs-gumroad-2026) (third-party)
- Payhip collects and remits EU and UK VAT on digital products, but you are on your own for US sales tax, Australian GST and similar. You remain seller of record, so chargebacks and disputes hit your own Stripe or PayPal account. Payouts go straight to your PayPal or Stripe; "those are the only 2 ways to get paid." — [Fungies Payhip review 2026](https://fungies.io/payhip-review-2026) (third-party)

**Paddle**
- Paddle sells to buyers in 200+ countries; balance currencies are USD, EUR, GBP, AUD and CAD. — [Paddle developer docs: supported currencies](https://developer.paddle.com/concepts/sell/supported-currencies); [Paddle supported countries](https://developer.paddle.com/concepts/sell/supported-countries-locales)
- A third-party gateway review says Paddle has "no restricted seller country list; approval is business-type and compliance based", with a $100 minimum monthly payout. Unverified. — [LearnWithHasan Paddle](https://learnwithhasan.com/payment-gateways/paddle/). An older ProfitWell/Paddle help page says payouts come by wire transfer or Payoneer; it predates Paddle's current platform. — [ProfitWell help](https://learn.profitwell.com/help/manage/get-paid/when-and-how-do-i-get-paid)

**Etsy (digital downloads)**
- Bosnia and Herzegovina is not on Etsy's list of countries eligible for Etsy Payments, including via Payoneer. Etsy's policy says that if Etsy Payments isn't available in your location, you may not be able to open a shop. — [Etsy Help: Countries eligible for Etsy Payments](https://help.etsy.com/hc/articles/115015710408); [Etsy Payments policy](https://www.etsy.com/legal/etsy-payments/)

**Stan Store**
- Stan doesn't let you connect an existing Stripe account. It creates a Stan-managed Stripe Custom account in "37 supported countries". Plans are $29 and $99/month. Payouts clear Stripe in 2–7 business days, then you cash out manually and wait another 24–48 hours (third-party). — [Cartmango Stan Store pricing 2026](https://cartmango.com/stan-store-pricing/)

**Skool**
- 2026 pricing: Hobby is $9/month with a 10% + $0.30 transaction fee (described as all-in); Pro is $99/month with 2.9% + $0.30 (≤$900) or 3.9% + $0.30 (larger payments). Sources conflict on whether Stripe processing is extra. Payouts are weekly via Stripe Express, in local currency (third-party). — [SchoolMaker Skool pricing 2026](https://www.schoolmaker.com/blog/skool-pricing); [Skoolprep payments explained](https://skoolprep.com/skool-payments-payouts-explained); [Learning Revolution](https://www.learningrevolution.net/skool-pricing/)

### Inferences
- MoR matters for a BiH seller because BiH is outside the EU. Selling B2C digital products to EU consumers yourself would generally mean registering for EU VAT through the non-Union OSS scheme. Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy and Polar (and Whop with its 2% tax option) take this burden off you. This is my inference from general EU VAT rules; I did not verify it with a source in this session.
- Payhip effectively needs PayPal for a BiH seller, because Stripe merchant accounts appear unavailable (Section 5). PayPal BiH payouts only go to Visa cards. Payhip is therefore a weaker option.
- Stan Store's model needs a Stan-managed Stripe Custom account in one of 37 countries. BiH is very likely not among them, given Stripe's merchant country coverage, but I could not see the list.
- Skool uses Stripe Express. Stripe's Connect docs list BA for Express accounts (Section 5), so Skool payouts to BiH may be possible via cross-border payouts. This is unverified.

### Gaps
- I could not research Kajabi, Sellfy, Ko-fi and Shopify digital (fees, MoR status, BiH payouts).
- I have no confirmation of Stan Store's 37-country list or Skool's payout-country list.
- I have not confirmed whether Gumroad's 10% + $0.50 includes card processing after it became an MoR.

## 5. Collecting payments from Bosnia and Herzegovina: Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer, Wise, platform payouts, workarounds, local rails, tax

### Takeaway
I found no evidence that Stripe offers standard merchant accounts to BiH-registered businesses. Stripe does list BA for Connect Express/Custom connected accounts (cross-border payouts), which is how Polar (and likely Skool) can pay BiH residents. PayPal BiH accounts can receive money, but withdrawals go only to Visa cards issued in BiH; Mastercard and Maestro are not supported and there is no direct bank withdrawal. The cleanest documented routes are Gumroad (direct bank deposit in BAM), Lemon Squeezy (BiH on its bank-payout list) and Polar (Stripe Connect Express, BiH listed). I could not confirm Wise or Payoneer accounts for BiH residents. Income must be declared: in FBiH, 10% income tax plus 4% health contributions, self-assessed within 5 days of receipt.

### Cited Findings
**Stripe**
- Stripe Tax supports collecting VAT in BiH on digital products, for remote sellers without a physical presence, with support dating from 12/18/2024. That is a tax-collection feature for foreign sellers, not merchant-account availability for BiH residents. — [Stripe docs: Collect tax in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries/europe/bosnia-and-herzegovina); [Stripe Tax supported countries](https://docs.stripe.com/tax/supported-countries)
- BA appears in Stripe's country list for Express and Custom connected accounts. Stripe notes that some countries are available only via cross-border payouts. — [Stripe docs: Connected account types](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/accounts); [Stripe cross-border payouts](https://docs.stripe.com/connect/cross-border-payouts). "BA" appears among supported_transfer_countries in the Country Specs API. — [Stripe API: Country Specs](https://stripe.com/docs/api/country_specs/list)

**Tax for a BiH-resident seller**
- FBiH: income from foreign clients is treated as self-employment income. The taxpayer self-assesses 10% income tax and 4% health insurance contributions, files form AMS-1035 within five days of receipt, and attaches proof of any foreign tax paid to avoid double taxation (Tax Administration of FBiH, June 2025). — [Paragraf.ba (18 Jun 2025)](https://www.paragraf.ba/dnevne-vijesti/18062025/18062025-vijest3.html); see also [Unija: freelancing rights and tax obligations FBiH](https://unija.com/bs/freelancing-prava-i-porezne-obaveze-fbih/). Rules differ in Republika Srpska and Brčko District.
- Banks must provide the Tax Administration with account turnover data on request. A past FBiH change triggered retroactive tax demands back to 2015, which is historical context. — [RSE: freelancers in BiH, contributions and tax](https://www.slobodnaevropa.org/a/freelance-bih-doprinosi-porez/31517840.html); [RSE: freelancers, foreign income](https://www.slobodnaevropa.org/a/freelanceri-bih-porez-inostranstvo/29405154.html)
- VAT: foreign providers of digital services to BiH consumers must register with ITA and charge 17% VAT. — [UINO (ITA) notice](https://www.uino.gov.ba/portal/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/33763); [VATCalc](https://www.vatcalc.com/bosnia-and-herzegovina/bosnia-herzegovina-vat-on-b2c-digital-services/); [Fiscal Requirements](https://www.fiscal-requirements.com/news/2203-bosnia-and-herzegovina-taxes-b2c-digital-services-for-non-resident-traders-with-vat). The registration threshold is reported as BAM 100,000 by one source and BAM 50,000 for remote digital sellers by another, which is a conflict. Exports are reportedly zero-rated. — [Kintsugi BiH VAT guide 2026](https://trykintsugi.com/sales-tax-guides/europe/bosnia-and-herzegovina); [Anrok BiH VAT guide](https://www.anrok.com/vat-software-digital-services/bosnia-and-herzegovina)

### Inferences
- Recommended primary rails for a BiH individual, based on documented support: (1) Gumroad, MoR with BAM bank payout; (2) Polar, MoR with Stripe Connect Express payout to a BiH bank; (3) Lemon Squeezy, MoR with BiH on its bank list, but check post-Stripe changes. Whop is a strong fourth option for communities and clipping, but BiH payout availability must be checked in the dashboard. PayPal works as a secondary rail, as long as you have a Visa card from a bank that accepts PayPal withdrawals.
- Because the MoR is the legal seller to the end customer, the BiH seller's income is a payout from a foreign platform. In FBiH that likely means the 10% + 4% self-assessment on each receipt (within 5 days). A local tax advisor should confirm whether to register as a sole proprietor (obrt/samostalna djelatnost) or a company, especially at volume.
- Platforms require the bank account to be in the seller's country of residence or registration (Gumroad, Polar, Whop). Any "foreign company" workaround (Estonia, US LLC, UK) therefore also needs a bank account in that country. It also creates questions about where the company is effectively managed and whether it has a permanent establishment in BiH, which can bring the income back into BiH tax. This is my inference; I have no source for it.

## 6. Risks: refunds, chargebacks, platform bans, "make money online" saturation, consumer protection

### Takeaway
The documented risks are: fees not refunded on refunds (Gumroad), chargeback liability falling on the seller when the platform isn't a full MoR (Payhip), content bans (PLR/resale on Payhip), country lock-out (Etsy), finite and verified-view clipping budgets, and tax visibility in BiH (banks report turnover on request). I found no sourced data on "make money online" saturation, chargeback rates or EU withdrawal rights for digital downloads; the search budget ran out.

### Cited Findings
- Gumroad does not return its fees when you refund a customer. — [checkoutpage.com Gumroad fees](https://checkoutpage.com/blog/gumroad-fees) (third-party)
- With a non-MoR storefront like Payhip, chargebacks and fraud disputes go through your own Stripe or PayPal account. A full MoR (Paddle, FastSpring) handles tax filings and takes on chargeback and fraud liability. — [Fungies Payhip review 2026](https://fungies.io/payhip-review-2026)
- Payhip reportedly bans copyrighted and resold PLR material, with account removal for violations; appeals are email-only and slow (single third-party assessment). — [Fungies Payhip review 2026](https://fungies.io/payhip-review-2026)
- Etsy may not let you open a shop at all if Etsy Payments isn't available in your country (BiH isn't listed). — [Etsy Payments policy](https://www.etsy.com/legal/etsy-payments/)
- Whop clipping budgets are finite and first come, first served, and views are verified over a tracking window before payout. — [OpusClip](https://www.opus.pro/blog/whop-content-rewards); [Clipping Programs](https://clippingprograms.com/clipping/)
- Instagram's 24-hour messaging window limits automated DM follow-ups. Breaking automation rules risks account restrictions. — [ManyChat: Instagram DM automation rules](https://manychat.com/blog/instagram-dm-automation-rules/)
- Most published funnel benchmarks (bump take rates, AOV lift) come from vendors selling funnel software. SamCart's own example shows about a 9.7% lift against a "30%+" headline. — [SamCart](https://www.samcart.com/blog/the-complete-guide-to-order-bumps)
- BiH tax authorities can get bank-account turnover data on request, so undeclared platform payouts are visible. — [RSE](https://www.slobodnaevropa.org/a/freelance-bih-doprinosi-porez/31517840.html)

### Inferences
- Selling "make money with AI" products to a "make money online" audience combines high refund and chargeback propensity with heightened platform and consumer-protection scrutiny. Using an MoR (Gumroad, Lemon Squeezy, Polar) shifts dispute handling to the platform but does not remove the account-ban risk if dispute rates get high. Concrete thresholds were not sourced.
- Single-rail dependence is a key risk for a BiH seller, because so few rails work. Having at least two working payout routes (for example Gumroad and Polar, or Lemon Squeezy and PayPal-to-Visa) reduces the impact of one account freeze.

### Gaps
- I found no chargeback or refund rate benchmarks for low-ticket digital products, and no platform dispute-rate thresholds (Gumroad, Whop, Lemon Squeezy, Polar).
- I could not research EU Consumer Rights Directive rules on the 14-day withdrawal right for digital content, including the consent-and-acknowledgment waiver at checkout, or how MoRs handle it.
- I have no data on saturation in AI or "make money online" digital products, or on FTC/EU actions against income-claim products.
- I could not research platform policies on AI-generated content, undisclosed AI influencers or multi-account operation.
